Why Better Information Leads to
Better Clinical Decisions

In This Article

Every piece of information matters. Better information improves continuity of care. Looking ahead
All Insights ↗

Every clinical decision depends on information.

Whether diagnosing an illness, prescribing treatment, or making a referral, healthcare professionals rely on accurate and timely information to deliver safe, effective care.

The more complete the picture, the more informed the decision.

Every piece of information matters.

A patient's allergies, medication history, previous diagnoses, laboratory results, and recent treatments all contribute to understanding their health.

When this information is unavailable, clinicians may have to rely on incomplete histories or repeat investigations before making decisions.

Access to relevant healthcare information provides valuable context that supports safer and more confident clinical decision-making.

Better information improves continuity of care.

Patients often receive care from more than one healthcare facility throughout their lives.

When healthcare information is available at the point of care, clinicians are better equipped to understand previous treatments, avoid unnecessary duplication, coordinate referrals, and make decisions based on a more complete picture of the patient's healthcare journey.

The result is more coordinated care and a better experience for both patients and healthcare professionals.

Looking ahead

Healthcare professionals should be able to focus on caring for patients, not searching for information.

As healthcare systems continue to evolve, improving access to trusted healthcare information will play an important role in supporting safer clinical decisions, stronger continuity of care, and better healthcare outcomes.
