Every healthcare interaction generates valuable information.

From consultations and laboratory results to diagnoses, prescriptions, and referrals, each encounter contributes to a patient's healthcare journey. Together, this information provides the context healthcare professionals need to make informed clinical decisions.

Too often, however, that information remains fragmented across different healthcare facilities.

When healthcare information is fragmented

Most patients receive care from more than one healthcare facility over their lifetime. They may visit a primary healthcare centre, a specialist clinic, a laboratory, a pharmacy, or a hospital.

While each facility contributes to the patient's health history, the information created during those encounters often remains where it was first recorded.

As a result, healthcare professionals may only have access to part of the patient's story when making important clinical decisions.

The impact on care

Fragmented healthcare information creates challenges across the healthcare system. Healthcare professionals may have to spend valuable time gathering information, repeat investigations because previous results are unavailable, or make decisions without a complete health history.

Patients may also experience delays in care, repeated questions during consultations, and interruptions in continuity of care when they seek treatment at different healthcare facilities or relocate to another city.

These challenges affect not only efficiency, but also the quality and coordination of care.

A better approach

Making trusted healthcare information available at the point of care helps healthcare professionals make more informed decisions with greater confidence.

Access to relevant healthcare information supports continuity of care, reduces unnecessary duplication, improves referrals, and enables healthcare providers to better understand a patient's healthcare journey.

The benefits extend beyond individual care. Responsibly anonymized healthcare information can also provide valuable insights for disease surveillance, population health, and health system planning.

Looking ahead

Healthcare is a continuous journey, but healthcare information often remains disconnected.

As healthcare systems continue to evolve, improving the availability of trusted healthcare information will be essential to supporting better clinical decisions, stronger continuity of care, and more resilient health systems.
