Healthcare Information Shouldn't
Stop at the Hospital Door

In This Article

The challenge Continuity of information supports continuity of care Looking ahead
All Insights ↗

Healthcare doesn't happen in one place.

Over the course of a lifetime, people receive care from different healthcare facilities for different needs. They may visit a primary healthcare centre, consult a specialist, undergo laboratory tests, collect medication from a pharmacy, or receive emergency care at a hospital.

Each encounter contributes to their healthcare journey.

The challenge

While healthcare journeys continue across multiple facilities, healthcare information often does not.

Important details such as previous diagnoses, laboratory results, medication history, and referrals may remain where they were first recorded, making them unavailable when patients seek care elsewhere.

This can leave healthcare professionals without the complete information they need to make well-informed decisions.

Continuity of information supports continuity of care

Continuity of care depends on more than referrals and follow-up appointments. It also depends on healthcare professionals having access to relevant healthcare information when patients move between healthcare facilities.

When trusted healthcare information is available at the point of care, clinicians are better equipped to understand a patient's health history, coordinate treatment, avoid unnecessary duplication, and make decisions with greater confidence.

This creates a more connected healthcare experience for both patients and healthcare professionals.

Looking ahead

As healthcare becomes increasingly interconnected, healthcare information should be available wherever patients receive care.

Making trusted healthcare information available at the point of care is an important step toward improving continuity of care, strengthening collaboration between healthcare facilities, and supporting better healthcare outcomes.
